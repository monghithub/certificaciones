## Task Statement 4.1

Explain the development of AI systems that are responsible.

### Objectives

1. **4.1.1** Identify features of responsible AI (for example, bias, fairness, inclusivity, robustness, safety, veracity).
2. **4.1.2** Explain how to use tools to identify features of responsible AI (for example, Amazon Bedrock Guardrails).
3. **4.1.3** Define responsible practices to select a model (for example, environmental considerations, sustainability).
4. **4.1.4** Identify legal risks of working with generative AI (GenAI) (for example, intellectual property infringement claims, biased model outputs, loss of customer trust, end user risk, hallucinations).
5. **4.1.5** Identify characteristics of datasets (for example, inclusivity, diversity, curated data sources, balanced datasets).
6. **4.1.6** Describe effects of bias and variance (for example, effects on demographic groups, inaccuracy, overfitting, underfitting).
7. **4.1.7** Describe tools to detect and monitor bias, trustworthiness, and truthfulness (for example, analyzing label quality, human audits, subgroup analysis).

## Task Statement 4.2

Recognize the importance of transparent and explainable models.

### Objectives

1. **4.2.1** Describe the differences between models that are transparent and explainable and models that are not transparent and explainable.
2. **4.2.2** Describe tools to identify transparent and explainable models (for example, Amazon SageMaker Model Cards, Amazon Bedrock Model Evaluations, open source models, data, licensing).
3. **4.2.3** Identify tradeoffs between model safety and transparency (for example, measure interpretability and performance).
4. **4.2.4** Describe principles of human-centered design for explainable AI (for example, user-feedback mechanisms, AI decision transparency).
